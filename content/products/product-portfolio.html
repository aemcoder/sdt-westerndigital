Hard Drive Storage FAQ

What drives are best for NAS systems?

WD Red PlusandWD Red Proare Western Digital's NAS-optimized hard drives.

  • WD Red Plusis ideal for home offices, small businesses, and everyday NAS workloads such as file sharing, backups, and media storage.
  • WD Red Prois designed for demanding multi-user and high-density NAS environments, featuring vibration protection, NAS-optimized firmware, enterprise-class reliability, and support for workloads up to550TB/year3.

ChooseWD Red Plusfor everyday NAS storage needs andWD Red Profor heavier workloads, larger NAS systems, and business-critical applications.

What is the best hard drive for surveillance?

WD PurpleandWD Purple Proare Western Digital's surveillance-optimized hard drives.

  • WD Purpleis ideal for everyday surveillance systems and continuous video recording.
  • WD Purple Prois designed for advanced surveillance environments with AI-enabled analytics, supporting up to64 HD camera streams and 32 concurrent AI streams.

Choose WD Purplefor standard surveillance deployments andWD Purple Profor larger systems that require advanced analytics, higher camera densities, and smart video capabilities.

To determine the right capacity for your surveillance system, use ourSurveillance Capacity Calculatorto estimate storage requirements based on your camera count, resolution, recording settings, and retention goals.

An HDD stores data on spinning magnetic platters, while an SSD uses flash memory with no moving parts. SSDs are much faster for booting and loading files, while HDDs deliver far more capacity per dollar. Many systems combine an SSD for speed with an HDD for high-capacity storage and backups.

Which WD drives support RAID configurations?

Western Digital offers several hard drives designed for RAID environments.

  • WD Red Plusis optimized for personal and small business NAS systems and RAID-enabled storage environments.
  • WD Red Prois purpose-built for demanding NAS and RAID environments, featuring NAS-optimized firmware, vibration protection, and workloads of up to 550TB/year3.
  • WD Goldis designed for enterprise servers, data centers, and business-critical RAID storage environments that require maximum reliability and 24/7 operation.

The best drive depends on your workload and environment, fromhome and small business NAS systemstoenterprise-class RAID storage.

To determine the usable storage capacity for your RAID configuration, use ourRAID Storage Calculator.

What drives are used in data centers?

UltrastarandWD Golddrives are designed for data center environments.

  • Ultrastardrives are engineered for cloud, hyperscale, AI, and enterprise data centers.
  • WD Golddrives are built for enterprise servers, data centers, and RAID storage running demanding 24/7 workloads.

Theseenterprise-class drivesare designed to deliver the reliability, scalability, and performance required for data center storage applications.

What is the difference between CMR and SMR hard drives?

CMR hard drives write data tracks side by side, helping deliver consistent write performance for active workloads, NAS systems, RAID arrays, and frequent data updates. SMR hard drives overlap data tracks to increase storage density and help lower cost per terabyte, but they can be slower under sustained write workloads. For NAS and RAID environments, CMR is typically the better fit, while SMR is better suited for archival or light, read-mostly storage.

What is UltraSMR technology?

UltraSMRis Western Digital's advanced shingled magnetic recording technology used in selectUltrastardata center hard drives. It is designed to increase storage density and deliver higher capacities, enabling up to20%+ more capacity compared to CMR-based designswhile helping data centers maximize storage efficiency.

  1. 1MB = 1 million bytes, 1GB = 1 billion bytes, 1TB = 1 trillion bytes. Actual user capacity may be less depending on operating environment.
  2. MTBF specifications are based on a sample population and are estimated by statistical measurements and acceleration algorithms under typical operating conditions.
  3. Workload Rate is defined as the amount of user data transferred to or from the hard drive. Workload Rate is annualized (TB transferred × (8760 / recorded power-on hours)). Workload Rate will vary depending on your hardware and software components and configurations.
